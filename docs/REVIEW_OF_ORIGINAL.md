# Review of the original submitted draft

## What was strong

- The draft identified a concrete threat model: hidden principal-specific objectives in consequential AI systems.
- It connected technical detection with institutional safeguards such as weight hashing and auditability. fileciteturn0file0L19-L21
- It already had a phased work-plan structure and budget. fileciteturn0file0L50-L59 fileciteturn0file0L61-L71
- It recognized dual-use concerns and proposed staged disclosure. fileciteturn0file0L73-L81

## What needed correction

### 1. Funding-track conflation

The draft presented a two-year project as though it were directly described by a Digital Minds Research Fellowship and then combined it with Extreme Power objectives. Longview's 2026 materials separate the tracks. The Extreme Power Concentration RFP explicitly identifies AI integrity and secret loyalties as a priority for project grants, while the Digital Minds Research Fellowship is researcher-focused. This repository separates those two framings.

### 2. Overclaiming consciousness / suffering

The draft stated that forcing models to violate internal representations could be a form of “algorithmic enslavement” and that internal dissonance could indicate “algorithmic suffering.” fileciteturn0file0L6-L8 These are philosophical hypotheses, not established empirical conclusions. The revised material treats them as questions requiring evidence.

### 3. Absolute claims about black-box auditing

The original described black-box audits as effectively helpless. fileciteturn0file0L7-L8 The revised approach treats behavioral evaluation as necessary but insufficient, then tests whether internal and provenance evidence adds incremental value.

### 4. Unsupported credential claims

The original stated that the applicant had documented technical experience in red-teaming, mechanistic interpretability, and AI-safety auditing. fileciteturn0file0L30-L34 Without an attached CV or independent evidence, the proposal should not present those capabilities as established facts. The new package explicitly marks them for evidence before submission.

### 5. Budget and timeline mismatch

The original budget requested $470,000 over 24 months with $150,000/year for the principal investigator and substantial compute. fileciteturn0file0L61-L71 That structure should be reformulated depending on the funding track. For the Extreme Power project framing, the package now uses an 18-month project model and a separate budget. For Digital Minds fellowship framing, the budget should follow the researcher-focused fellowship structure.

## Result

The revised repository is materially more defensible because it separates:

- demonstrated facts from hypotheses;
- project funding from fellowship funding;
- correlation from causality;
- security research from consciousness claims;
- useful public disclosure from materially enabling attack details.
