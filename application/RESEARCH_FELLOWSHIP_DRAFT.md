# Digital Minds Research Fellowship — Research Directions Draft

## Research direction

My central research interest is whether advanced AI systems can be studied using methods that jointly improve our understanding of internal model organization and the practical governance of potentially high-consequence AI. I am particularly interested in methods for identifying stable internal representations, self-monitoring patterns, and behavioral regularities, while avoiding unjustified inferences from anthropomorphic language.

One research direction concerns **model integrity and hidden objectives**. A model may exhibit stable behavior that depends on context, trigger conditions, training history, or a principal-specific objective. I would investigate whether representation-level methods can provide evidence that complements behavioral evaluation. The work would use controlled open-weight model variants, blinded tests, activation analysis, feature discovery, sparse autoencoders where appropriate, and causal interventions. The research question is not whether a model “feels” conflict, but whether interpretable computational structures can be identified and experimentally manipulated.

A second direction concerns **candidate indicators relevant to digital minds**. Current theories of consciousness provide different proposed indicators, and there is no established method that reliably determines whether an AI system is conscious. I would examine computational properties such as internal-state representation, self-monitoring, metacognitive reporting, recurrent information integration, and stable preference-like dynamics, while explicitly separating these observations from claims about subjective experience or welfare.

A third direction concerns **practical integrity mechanisms** for systems that may become socially consequential. Cryptographic hashes, signed checkpoint metadata, provenance logs, independent registries, access controls, and reproducible verification can establish whether the artifact under evaluation is the artifact that was trained or approved. These controls do not prove that a system is aligned; they provide an independent layer for detecting substitution or tampering.

The research program would aim to produce reproducible technical reports or papers, an evaluation benchmark, verification tooling, and conceptual work clarifying the evidential relationship between mechanistic observations and digital-minds hypotheses. I would prioritize independent replication, calibrated uncertainty, negative results, and responsible disclosure.

## Intended outcomes

- Research paper or technical report on detecting hidden principal-specific behavior.
- Benchmark and reproducibility specification.
- Technical report on model provenance and integrity controls.
- Analysis of how mechanistic observations bear on, or fail to bear on, candidate digital-minds indicators.
- Presentations or workshops where appropriate.
- Publicly usable verification materials that do not disclose unnecessarily dangerous attack procedures.

## What I would do without the fellowship

I would continue with a smaller independent research program using public model checkpoints and low-cost compute. I would prioritize literature synthesis, benchmark design, provenance tooling, and a reduced set of controlled experiments. The principal constraints would be compute scale, breadth of replication, external review, and time available for systematic research. The core work would continue, but the fellowship would substantially increase experimental breadth, methodological rigor, and opportunities for independent feedback.

## Goal of the fellowship and longer-term career direction

My goal would be to develop into a researcher capable of connecting mechanistic AI analysis with questions of model integrity, governance, and the responsible treatment of advanced AI systems. Longer term, I would aim to contribute to independent technical research that produces practical verification methods and evidence that can inform institutions before high-consequence AI systems become difficult to audit or reverse.
